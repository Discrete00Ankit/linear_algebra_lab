1 What is a Point, Line, and Plane?

In $\mathbb{R}^n$, a point is an exact location in space, devoid of size or width: an ordered coordinate pair $(x, y)$. A line is a 1D continuous set of points extending infinitely in opposite directions. A plane is a flat 2D surface extending infinitely with zero thickness.

Linear Equation in Two Variables

An algebraic equation that can be written in standard form: $$ax + by = c$$ where $x$ and $y$ are indeterminates, $a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}$, and $a$ and $b$ are not both zero.

🤔 Stop & Ponder: What makes an equation "Linear"?

Why is $x + y = 2$ called linear? Compare the uniformity of values of $y$ after uniform steps along $x$. Is $\sin(x) + \sin(y) = 1$ linear?

Key Conceptual Takeaway

For $x + y = 2$, the rate of change $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = -1$ is strictly constant everywhere. In contrast, for $\sin(x) + \sin(y) = 1$, the slope fluctuates continuously, producing curves rather than straight lines.

🎯 What to Explore & Predict Before Interacting

• Action: Switch equation choices and slide the step $\\Delta x$. Observe how the tangent slope behaves.

💡 Core Geometric & Algebraic Takeaway

• Linearity Criterion: A linear equation in two variables has the standard form $ax + by = c$. Variables appear strictly to the first power without products, powers, or roots.
• Geometry of Linearity: The graph of a linear equation is always a flat, straight 1D line in $\\mathbb{R}^2$ with constant slope $m = -a/b$.

2 System of Linear Equations in 2 Variables

A system of linear equations is a collection of two or more linear equations involving the same set of variables:

$$\begin{aligned} a_1x + b_1y &= c_1 \\ a_2x + b_2y &= c_2 \end{aligned}$$

A solution is an ordered pair $(x, y)$ that satisfies every equation simultaneously.

🎯 What to Explore & Predict Before Interacting

• Predict: If you change the slope $m_1$ of Line 1 to match the slope $m_2$ of Line 2, will the lines intersect at a point?
• Action: Slide slopes $m_1, m_2$ and intercepts $c_1, c_2$. Observe where the unique intersection point $(x^*, y^*)$ travels.

💡 Core Geometric & Algebraic Takeaway

• System Solution Point: Solving a $2 \\times 2$ linear system means finding the exact coordinate point $(x^*, y^*)$ where both linear constraint lines cross.
• Determinant Connection: When slopes differ ($m_1 \\neq m_2$), the lines are non-parallel, guaranteeing a single unique intersection point.

🎯 What to Explore & Predict Before Interacting

• Predict: Under what specific condition do two lines have infinitely many solutions versus zero solutions?
• Action: Select presets: Unique Solution, Coincident Lines (infinitely many), and Parallel Lines (no solution). Observe the matrix coefficients.

💡 Core Geometric & Algebraic Takeaway

• Trichotomy in 2D: Two lines either cross at 1 point (different slopes), never cross (same slope, different intercepts $\\implies$ 0 solutions), or overlap everywhere (same slope and same intercept $\\implies$ $\\infty$ solutions).

Theorem 1 Visual Matrix: The Three Geometric States in ℝ²

Every 2D linear system $a_1x + b_1y = c_1$ and $a_2x + b_2y = c_2$ falls strictly into one of three universal solvability categories:

1. Unique Solution Consistent · Independent
Condition: $\frac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \frac{b_1}{b_2}$ (Unequal slopes)
Rank: $\text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}([A|b]) = 2$
Geometry: Lines cross at exactly 1 unique coordinate point.
2. No Solution Inconsistent · Independent
Condition: $\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2}$ (Equal slopes, distinct intercepts)
Rank: $\text{rank}(A) = 1 < \text{rank}([A|b]) = 2$
Geometry: Parallel lines never meet anywhere in $\mathbb{R}^2$.
3. Infinite Solutions Consistent · Dependent
Condition: $\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}$ (Proportional equations)
Rank: $\text{rank}(A) = \text{rank}([A|b]) = 1 < 2$
Geometry: Lines lie directly on top of each other.

3 Moving to 3 Variables: What Does $x + y + z = 1$ Represent?

In two dimensions, $x + y = 2$ represents a 1D straight line. When we introduce a third indeterminate variable:

$$x + y + z = 1$$

Geometrically, this equation defines a flat 2D plane slicing through 3-dimensional space ($\mathbb{R}^3$).

Consider the three-equation system from Sheet 1:

$$\begin{cases} x + y + z = 1 \\ 2x + 2y + 2z = -1 \\ x + y - z = 0 \end{cases}$$

Finding a solution means finding where all three flat planes share common coordinate points simultaneously.

The 6 Geometric Configurations of 3 Planes in ℝ³

When 3 flat planes slice through 3-dimensional space, they must satisfy one of six geometric geometries:

1. Unique Point 1 Solution
Planes meet at a single corner vertex $(x, y, z)$. $\det(A) \neq 0$.
2. Common Line ∞ Solutions
All 3 planes intersect along an entire line (like pages of an open book).
3. Triangular Prism 0 Solutions
Pairwise lines are parallel; no single point lies on all 3 planes simultaneously.
4. Two Parallel + Transversal 0 Solutions
Two parallel floor/ceiling planes sliced diagonally by a third plane.
5. Three Parallel Slices 0 Solutions
Three parallel planes stacked like shelves with zero shared points.
6. Coincident Planes ∞ Solutions
Two or all three equations represent the exact same plane.

4 Theorem 1: Trichotomy for Linear Systems

Theorem 1 (Trichotomy for Linear Systems)

Every system of linear equations has either:

  1. No solutions (Inconsistent)
  2. Exactly one solution (Consistent)
  3. Infinitely many solutions (Consistent)
🤔 Stop & Ponder: Why can a system never have exactly 2 solutions?

If two distinct points satisfy a linear system, what must be true about the entire straight line connecting them?

Geometric Proof

If points $\mathbf{p}_1$ and $\mathbf{p}_2$ both satisfy the equations, then any weighted point $t\mathbf{p}_1 + (1-t)\mathbf{p}_2$ also satisfies every plane constraint, immediately generating an entire continuum of infinitely many solutions!

🎯 What to Explore & Predict Before Interacting

• Predict: Can a linear system ever have exactly 2 solutions or 5 solutions? Why or why not?
• Action: Drag the slider parameters across the 2D plane to test all possible intersection topologies.

💡 Core Geometric & Algebraic Takeaway

• Theorem 1.1 (Trichotomy): A system of linear equations over $\\mathbb{R}$ has either 0, 1, or infinitely many solutions. Exactly 2 or any other finite count is geometrically impossible because straight lines and flat planes cannot curve back to intersect again.

5 Why Linear Algebra? The Matrix Notation & Dual-Verse

Why do we package linear equations into $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$? Because our visual intuition fails past 3 dimensions, but matrix algebra scales effortlessly to thousands of dimensions.

$$\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}}_{A} \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{x}} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{b}}$$

Rewritten column by column, this gives the Column Picture:

$$x \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} + y \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

📍 The Row Picture

  • Reads across rows as constraint lines/planes.
  • Variables $(x, y)$ are coordinates of a single point.
  • Objective: Find where lines cross.

🎯 The Column Picture

  • Reads down columns as vector arrows.
  • Variables $(x, y)$ are scaling dials that stretch vectors.
  • Objective: Combine columns head-to-tail to hit target $\mathbf{b}$.
🎯 What to Explore & Predict Before Interacting

• Predict: How does the Row Picture view of $A\\mathbf{x} = \\mathbf{b}$ differ from the Column Picture view?
• Action: Toggle between the Row Verse (intersecting lines) and the Column Verse (vector addition head-to-tail). Drag coefficients and watch both views update in real time.

💡 Core Geometric & Algebraic Takeaway

• Row Picture vs Column Picture:
- Row Picture: Finding the intersection point of hyperplane constraints ($n$ equations).
- Column Picture: Finding weights $x_1, x_2$ to combine column vectors: $x_1 \\mathbf{a}_1 + x_2 \\mathbf{a}_2 = \\mathbf{b}$.
• Both pictures represent the exact same underlying algebra!
