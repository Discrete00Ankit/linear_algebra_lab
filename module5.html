1 Why Factor Matrices? Storing Elimination History

When you solve $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ by hand, Gaussian Elimination overwrites the original numbers in $A$ until it becomes upper triangular $U$. But what if tomorrow you are given a new target vector $\mathbf{b}_{\text{new}}$ with the exact same coefficient matrix $A$? Re-running the entire $O(n^3)$ elimination from scratch is computationally wasteful.

The Philosophy of Factorization

Matrix factorization decomposes a single complicated matrix $A$ into a product of simpler structured matrices (such as triangular matrices): $$A = L \cdot U$$ • $L$ (Lower Triangular): Records the history of every elimination step and multiplier ($L_{ii} = 1$).
• $U$ (Upper Triangular): The final echelon form holding the pivots on its diagonal.
Once factored, solving for any new right-hand side takes only $O(n^2)$ back- and forward-substitutions!

🤔 Stop & Ponder: Why do we multiply on the Left for Row Operations?

Recall from Module 2 that multiplying a matrix on the left combines rows, while multiplying on the right combines columns. To perform an elementary row operation on $A$, which side must our elementary matrix $E$ act on?

Left Action on Rows

Every row of the product $EA$ is a linear combination of the rows of $A$. Hence, row operations are strictly pre-multiplications ($E \cdot A$).

2 The Elementary Matrix $E_{ij}$ & The Inverse Undo Engine

An Elementary Matrix $E$ is obtained by applying a single Elementary Row Operation to the Identity Matrix $I$.

Theorem 5.1: The Elimination Matrix Structure

To subtract $m_{ji}$ times Row $i$ from Row $j$, the elementary matrix $E_{ji}$ has: $$E_{ji} = I \text{ with entry } (j, i) = -m_{ji}$$ Its inverse $E_{ji}^{-1}$, which undoes the subtraction by adding $m_{ji}$ times Row $i$ back, is simply: $$E_{ji}^{-1} = I \text{ with entry } (j, i) = +m_{ji}$$

💡 Core Geometric & Algebraic Takeaway

• Reversibility Built-in: Because every legal row operation is reversible, every elementary matrix $E$ is strictly invertible ($E^{-1}$ always exists).

• Sign Reversal: The inverse of an elimination matrix $E_{ji}$ flips the sign of the off-diagonal entry from $-m_{ji}$ to $+m_{ji}$.

4 The $A = LU$ Decomposition: Zero Crosstalk

During Gaussian Elimination, we apply a sequence of elimination matrices to reduce $A$ to upper triangular $U$:

$$E_{32} E_{31} E_{21} A = U$$

Multiplying by their inverses in reverse order moves all elimination steps to the other side:

$$A = (E_{21}^{-1} E_{31}^{-1} E_{32}^{-1}) U = L \cdot U$$
The Miracle of Zero Crosstalk in L

When multiplying $E$'s together ($E_{32} E_{31} E_{21}$), earlier operations interfere with subsequent rows (multiplier crosstalk). But in the inverse product: $$L = E_{21}^{-1} E_{31}^{-1} E_{32}^{-1} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ l_{21} & 1 & 0 \\ l_{31} & l_{32} & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$ Every multiplier $l_{ji}$ drops directly into position $(j, i)$ with zero distortion!

🎯 What to Explore: 2×2 LU Sandbox (Notebook 2 Cell 37)

• Adjust entry $a$ (pivot 1) and entry $c$. Observe how $l_{21} = c/a$ is stored in $L$, while the new pivot $u_{22} = d - (c/a)b$ appears in $U$.

• What happens if $a = 0$? Notice how zero pivot prevents standard $LU$ without row swap.

💡 Core Geometric & Algebraic Takeaway

• For any $2 \times 2$ matrix with $a \neq 0$: $$L = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ c/a & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad U = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ 0 & d - (c/a)b \end{bmatrix}$$ Multiplying $L \cdot U$ reconstructs entry-by-entry the original matrix $A$.
