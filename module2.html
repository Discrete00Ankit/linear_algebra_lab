2 The Order Paradox & Dimension Compatibility

In scalar math, multiplication is commutative ($3 \times 5 = 5 \times 3$). In linear algebra, matrix multiplication is non-commutative ($AB \neq BA$). Order dictates compatibility and life-or-death:

$$(m \times \mathbf{n}) \times (\mathbf{p} \times q)$$

Each row in $A$ has length $n$. Each column in $B$ has height $p$. If $n \neq p$, entries run out of partners during the dot product!

🎯 What to Explore & Predict Before Interacting

• Predict: If matrix $A$ is $2 \\times 3$ and matrix $B$ is $3 \\times 4$, does $AB$ exist? Does $BA$ exist?
• Action: Adjust the row and column dimension sliders for matrices $A$ and $B$. Observe when the dimensional block diagram connects or collides.

💡 Core Geometric & Algebraic Takeaway

• Inner Dimension Compatibility: For the product $AB$ to exist, the number of columns in $A$ must equal the number of rows in $B$: $(m \\times \\mathbf{n}) \\times (\\mathbf{n} \\times p) = m \\times p$. In general, $AB \\neq BA$.

4 Binary Images: What Does Matrix Multiplication Actually Do to an Image?

A digital monochrome screen displays pictures as a grid of pixels: a binary matrix $X$ where 1 is a shaded black pixel and 0 is white background.

The Positional Rule of Matrix Multipliers

• Left multiplication ($P \cdot X$) acts strictly on the horizontal rows (vertical motion: up, down, upside-down flip).
• Right multiplication ($X \cdot Q$) acts strictly on the vertical columns (horizontal motion: left, right, mirror flip).

🎯 What to Explore & Predict Before Interacting

• Predict: Does multiplying an image matrix by a shift matrix on the Left shift the pixels horizontally or vertically?
• Action: Apply Shift Up, Shift Down, Shift Left, and Shift Right transformation matrices to the binary icon.

💡 Core Geometric & Algebraic Takeaway

• Left vs Right Matrix Actions: Multiplying on the Left operates strictly on horizontal rows (vertical motion: up/down). Multiplying on the Right operates strictly on vertical columns (horizontal motion: left/right).

5 From One Recipe to a Recipe Book: Matrix-Matrix Multiplication ($AB$)

In the tea kitchen, we brewed a single cup using a single recipe vector $\mathbf{x}$ ($A \mathbf{x}$). What happens when a canteen prepares an entire menu of different dishes?

🏺 The Profile Matrix $A$

Rows record measured nutritional properties; columns represent ingredients:
• Row 1: Cost (₹)
• Row 2: Calories (kcal)
• Row 3: Protein (g)
• Columns: Rice, Paneer, Vegetables

📖 The Recipe Book Matrix $B$

Multiple recipe column vectors placed side by side:
• Column 1: Dish 1 (Rice & Veg Bowl)
• Column 2: Dish 2 (Paneer Fried Rice)
• Column 3: Dish 3 (Paneer Stack)

💡 Two Dual Views of the Exact Same Product $AB$:

• Columns of $AB$: Each column represents one finished dish evaluated across all properties: $A \cdot \mathbf{b}_j$.
• Rows of $AB$: Each row tracks one property across the entire menu: $\text{Row}_i(A) \cdot B$.
• Dimensional Match: $(\text{properties} \times \text{ingredients}) \times (\text{ingredients} \times \text{dishes}) = (\text{properties} \times \text{dishes})$.

Mathematical Invariants: Can Every Shape Be Reached by Permutations Alone?

Suppose you are permitted to use only row-permutation and column-permutation matrices (no zero-inserting shifts). Can you transform the $2 \times 2$ center block into a plus shape?

🛡️ The Rigorous Invariant Proof

• Center Block $X_0$ row counts: $[0, 2, 2, 0]$ (Multiset: $\{0, 0, 2, 2\}$).
• Plus-shaped target row counts: $[0, 1, 2, 1]$ or $[1, 2, 1, 0]$ (Multiset contains $1$s).
• Conclusion: Permutation matrices merely reorder rows and columns. They can never alter the multiset of row sums or column sums. Therefore, it is mathematically impossible to create the plus shape via permutations alone!

Scaling Up: The $8 \times 8$ Image Challenge

To shift a centered $4 \times 4$ block upward by $k$ rows and leftward by $k$ columns in an $8 \times 8$ frame:
• $P_{\uparrow k} = U^k$ (shifts rows up by $k$ on the left).
• $Q_{\leftarrow k} = L^k$ (shifts columns left by $k$ on the right).
